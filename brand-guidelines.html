01

Brand Architecture

Two distinct brands, one ecosystem. Tommy Arnold Productions is the artist brand — dark, cinematic, client-facing. ScoreSchool is the educator brand — warm, approachable, student-facing. They share a founder but never share a visual identity.

Tommy Arnold Productions

The Artist

Custom scores for film, television, and games. Horror/suspense specialty with range across all genres. The work speaks — the brand frames it.

Audience: Film directors, music editors, game developers, music supervisors

Mood: Dark, cinematic, industrial, atmospheric, confident

Tagline: Scores That Serve the Story

ScoreSchool

The Mentor

Education and tools for aspiring film and game composers. Practical, no-gatekeeping approach. Built by someone who's been through it.

Audience: Aspiring composers, career-changers, self-taught musicians

Mood: Warm, knowledgeable, structured, aspirational, inviting

Tagline: Score Your First Scene

Spacing & Layout

Consistent spacing creates rhythm. Both brands use an 8px base grid. Generous whitespace is a key part of the premium feel — don't crowd elements.

Base Grid: 8px

Layout Rules

Do

  • Use 80px minimum section padding on desktop
  • Give headlines 48px+ bottom margin before body text
  • Keep body text max-width at 680px for readability
  • Use 16-24px gap between grid items
  • Allow elements to breathe — negative space is power

Don't

  • Cram content edge-to-edge
  • Use inconsistent spacing between sections
  • Let body text span full width on desktop
  • Stack multiple CTAs without breathing room
  • Use borders where spacing would suffice

Voice & Tone — TAP

Tommy Arnold Productions speaks with artist energy — confident, atmospheric, intentional. Every word should feel like it belongs in a dark room with dramatic lighting.

AttributeDescriptionExample
ConfidentState what you do without hedging. No "I think" or "maybe.""I score the moments that make your skin crawl."
AtmosphericEvoke feeling, not just information. Use sensory language."Tension and texture, woven into every frame."
IntentionalEverything has purpose. No filler words or generic phrases."Custom score" — never "background music."
DirectRespect the reader's time. Get to the point."Let's talk about your project." — not "Feel free to reach out anytime!"
// EMAIL OUTREACH — DIRECTOR PITCH
"I watched the trailer for [Film] — the tension in the interrogation scene is exactly the kind of work I love to score. I specialize in horror and suspense, and I think the score could push those moments even further. Here's my reel: [link]"
// SOCIAL — INSTAGRAM / TIKTOK
"This cue started as a single cello note and a room full of silence. 47 layers later, it became the sound of dread. Here's how."
// WEBSITE — SERVICES PAGE
"Every film deserves a score that doesn't just accompany the story — it transforms it. I work directly with directors to uncover the emotional core of each scene and build custom music around it."

Copywriting Rules

Words to use, words to avoid, and rules that apply across both brands.

TAP — Use These

  • "Custom score" (never "background music")
  • "Tension and texture"
  • "I score the unsettling moments"
  • "Dramatic. Dark. Intentional."
  • Director/project-specific language in outreach
  • "Scores That Serve the Story"

TAP — Never Use

  • "Background music" or "stock music"
  • Generic pitch language
  • "I can do any genre" (lead with specialty)
  • Offering to send additional samples after reel
  • "Just reaching out" or "touching base"
  • Exclamation marks in outreach emails

ScoreSchool — Use These

  • "Here's the process"
  • "You don't need permission to start"
  • "Score your first scene"
  • "Let's break this down"
  • "Step by step"
  • "Real-world workflow"

ScoreSchool — Never Use

  • "Easy" (undermines the craft)
  • "Just" (minimizing)
  • Academic jargon without explanation
  • "Content creator" when referring to composers
  • "AMAZING OPPORTUNITY" energy
  • Anything gatekeep-y or elitist

Web & Digital

Design patterns for websites, landing pages, and digital products. Inspired by Spitfire Audio's premium product design and modern dark web aesthetics.

TAP Website Patterns

Design Principles

  • Full-bleed cinematic hero sections with atmospheric texture
  • Wide letter-spaced headlines (6-8px spacing)
  • Generous padding (80px+ between sections)
  • Subtle texture/grain on backgrounds (not flat black)
  • Audio player as a featured section, not an afterthought
  • Testimonials with real photos — social proof front and center
  • One clear CTA per section

Technical Specs

  • Max content width: 1200px
  • Body text: 16px Inter, max-width 680px
  • Section padding: 80px vertical, 40-80px horizontal
  • Border radius: 12-16px for cards
  • Border color: #1A1A1A (subtle, never harsh)
  • Hover states: Neon green glow or border shift
  • Transitions: 200ms ease for all interactions

ScoreSchool Website Patterns

Design Principles

  • Navy backgrounds with warm gold accents
  • Clean, readable layouts — not overly dark
  • Course modules displayed as clear, numbered cards
  • Testimonials and social proof near CTAs
  • Email signup as a prominent element (lead magnet)
  • Progress indicators and "what you'll learn" lists
  • Warm, inviting imagery (studio shots, DAW screenshots)

Sales Page Structure (SYFS)

  • Hero: Pain point → Promise → CTA
  • Social proof: Director testimonials
  • Curriculum: Module-by-module breakdown
  • About: Your story + credentials (not a resume)
  • Pricing: Clear tiers, early bird pricing
  • FAQ: Objection handling
  • Final CTA: Urgency + guarantee

Digital Products

All digital products sold through Payhip. Product cover art should look premium — think Spitfire Audio product cards, not clip-art PDFs.

Product Cover Design Rules

Do

  • Use brand-consistent cover art (dark bg for TAP, navy for SS)
  • Bold title in Montserrat or Raleway
  • Consistent sizing across all product listings
  • Include a subtle "ScoreSchool" or "TAP" brand mark
  • Use mockups for digital products (iPad, screen, book)

Don't

  • Use different design styles per product
  • Forget the brand mark
  • Use low-contrast text on covers
  • Mix TAP and ScoreSchool branding on one product
  • Use stock photography as the main visual
13

Master Do's & Don'ts

Cross-brand rules that apply everywhere, every time.

Always

  • Lead with TAP green (#5DD62C) as the accent — it's your signature
  • Use wide letter-spacing on TAP headings (minimum 3px)
  • Keep ScoreSchool warm and approachable (gold, navy, readable)
  • Maintain generous whitespace — premium = breathing room
  • Use real imagery (studio shots, DAW screenshots, your face)
  • Match voice to brand (artist energy for TAP, mentor energy for SS)
  • Include a CTA in every piece of content
  • Keep the "//" mark in neon green

Never

  • Mix TAP and ScoreSchool visual languages
  • Use neon green in ScoreSchool materials
  • Use warm gold in TAP materials (except extended palette sparingly)
  • Sound corporate — both brands are personal
  • Use stock photography as primary imagery
  • Stretch, rotate, or recolor the logo
  • Accept projects under $1,000 or do spec work
  • Offer to send additional samples when the reel has been shared
