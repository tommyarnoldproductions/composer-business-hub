05 — DESIGN ELEMENTS

Visual Language

Textures, imagery style, icon approach, and graphic elements that define each brand's visual identity.

TAP Visual Elements

Photography Style

Dark, atmospheric close-ups. Instruments in shadow, dramatic lighting. Think Spitfire product shots — cinematic, moody, high contrast. Never bright or casual.

Graphic Textures

Film grain overlays, waveform visualizations, audio spectrograms. Subtle noise textures on dark backgrounds. VHS/CRT scan lines for darker projects.

Icon Style

Thin line icons (1.5px stroke), monochrome green or white on dark. No filled/solid icons. Musical notation, waveforms, film strip elements.

Layout Approach

Full-bleed hero images, generous negative space, bold type overlaid on imagery. Grid-based with asymmetric compositions. Spitfire-style product pages.

ScoreSchool Visual Elements

Photography Style

Warm, educational. Behind-the-scenes studio shots, DAW screenshots, hands on keyboards. Approachable but professional. Think Crow Hill's maker energy.

Graphic Textures

Infographic-style diagrams (like Crow Hill cheat sheets), orchestration charts, waveform UI elements. Clean data visualization aesthetic on dark navy.

Icon Style

Outlined icons with slightly thicker stroke (2px). Cream or gold on navy. Musical education symbols — treble clefs, piano keys, film reels, play buttons.

Layout Approach

Card-based content organization. Module/lesson structure. Progress indicators. More structured grid than TAP. Generous padding, warm feel.

06 — BRAND RULES

Do's & Don'ts

Key guidelines to maintain brand consistency across all touchpoints.

Tommy Arnold Productions

Do

  • Use ALL CAPS for headlines with wide letter-spacing
  • Default to dark backgrounds (#0F0F0F or #1A1A1A)
  • Use green (#5DD62C) sparingly as accent — never as background
  • Keep photography dark, atmospheric, and cinematic
  • Use generous negative space — let content breathe
  • Apply film grain or noise textures subtly
  • Link to ReelCrafter reel — never send additional samples

Don't

  • Use light/white backgrounds — ever
  • Mix too many accent colors in one piece
  • Use rounded, playful, or bubbly typefaces
  • Use stock photos with bright, corporate lighting
  • Center-align body text (left-align always)
  • Use emojis in professional outreach
  • Crowd layouts — if in doubt, remove elements

ScoreSchool

Do

  • Use warm, inviting language — "you'll learn" not "students will"
  • Navy backgrounds with gold accents for premium feel
  • Title Case is OK for ScoreSchool (not just ALL CAPS)
  • Use infographic/diagram elements for educational content
  • Show real DAW screenshots and studio environments
  • Use DM Sans for body — warmer than Inter
  • Card-based layouts for course modules and lessons

Don't

  • Use dark/intense imagery on ScoreSchool content
  • Make it look too corporate or institutional
  • Use TAP's neon green — gold is ScoreSchool's accent
  • Overcomplicate layouts — clarity is key for education
  • Use jargon without explanation in student-facing content
  • Mix ScoreSchool and TAP branding on the same page
  • Use pure white (#FFF) — use warm cream (#F5F0E8) instead
