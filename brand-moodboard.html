06 — Design Patterns from Your Inspiration

Patterns to Adopt

Wide Letter-Spaced Headlines HIGH

TAP

Every Spitfire Audio page uses ultra-wide letter spacing on display type. Your current site has this partially — push it further. "TOMMY ARNOLD" should command the screen with 6-8px letter spacing, heavy weight, all caps.

Full-Bleed Cinematic Hero HIGH

TAP

Your "Abstract" screenshot and Spitfire product pages show the power of a cinematic hero that fills the viewport. Consider a dark atmospheric video loop or moody textured background instead of pure black void.

Glassmorphism / Frosted Cards MEDIUM

BOTH

Your pricing card screenshot uses frosted glass cards on dark backgrounds. This is a modern UI pattern that could elevate both your service cards (TAP) and course pricing (ScoreSchool).

Textured Backgrounds HIGH

TAP

NIN album art + British Drama Toolkit use distressed, smoky, glitch textures as backgrounds. Your testimonials section already has a subtle texture — extend this language across sections for depth and atmosphere.

Gold + Black for Premium MEDIUM

SCORESCHOOL

Wave Remedy Studios shows black + gold done right. ScoreSchool's navy + gold palette should lean into this premium educational feel — warm gold accents on deep navy, not yellow on light backgrounds.

Icon-Based Service Sections MEDIUM

TAP

Spitfire's Mercury page and Wave Remedy use clean line icons to break up service descriptions. Your Services page currently relies on stock-feeling photos — custom line icons would feel more intentional and premium.

Product Grid Layout MEDIUM

SCORESCHOOL

Spitfire's shop grid and Crow Hill's bundles page show how to display digital products as premium items with strong cover art, clear pricing, and add-to-cart actions. Apply this to your Payhip store or a Squarespace products page.

Iconic Brand Mark / Skull Motif MEDIUM

TAP / PUBLISHING CO

The skull logo and low-poly skull in your inspiration suggest interest in an iconic secondary mark. This could work as the publishing company logo, a favicon, or a content watermark. Worth exploring alongside your "//" mark.
